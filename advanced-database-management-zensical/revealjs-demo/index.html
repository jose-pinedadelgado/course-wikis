Part 1: Why Data Warehouses?

The Problem with OLTP for Analytics

Your CEO Asks...

"Show me revenue by department, by quarter, for the last 3 years — and compare it to last year."

In your normalized 3NF database:

  • 5+ JOINs across normalized tables
  • Aggregations across millions of rows
  • Competes with real-time transactions
  • Takes minutes to hours
Wrong tool for the job. You need a system designed for analysis.

Two Worlds of Data

"The operational systems are where you put the data in, and the DW/BI system is where you get the data out."
— Kimball

SchemaNormalized (3NF)Denormalized (Star)

Part 2: Star Schema Design

Kimball's 4-Step Process

The 4 Steps

StepQuestionHospital Example
1. Business ProcessWhat activity generates events?Patient appointments
2. Declare the GrainWhat is ONE row?One appointment
3. Identify DimensionsWho/what/where/when?Patient, Doctor, Date, Dept
4. Identify FactsWhat numbers to measure?Cost, wait time, Rx count
⚠️ Kimball's #1 warning: Never skip Step 2. If the grain isn't defined, the design rests on quicksand.

Star Schema Anatomy


                    ┌──────────────┐
                    │  dim_date    │   ← DIMENSION
                    └──────┬───────┘     (descriptive context)
                           │
  ┌──────────────┐  ┌──────┴──────────────┐  ┌──────────────┐
  │ dim_patient  │──┤  fact_appointments  │──┤ dim_department│

                    └────────────────────┘

Called a "star" because dimensions radiate from the central fact table.

Three Types of Facts

TypeCan SUM across...Example
AdditiveALL dimensions ✅Revenue, quantity, cost
Semi-additiveSome (not time) ⚠️Account balance

💡 Kimball's rule: "Ratio of the sums, not sum of the ratios."

The Unit Price Trap

You sold 1 widget at $1.00 and 4 widgets at $0.50 each.

QuantityUnit PriceRevenue
Sale 11$1.00$1.00
Sale 24$0.50$2.00
Total5???$3.00

❌ Wrong: ($1.00 + $0.50) / 2 = $0.75

✅ Correct: $3.00 / 5 = $0.60

The Date Dimension

Every star schema needs one. Pre-populate 10-20 years (~7,300 rows).


CREATE TABLE dim_date (
    date_key       NUMBER PRIMARY KEY,  -- 20260318
    full_date      DATE NOT NULL,
    day_of_week    VARCHAR2(10),        -- 'Wednesday'
    month_name     VARCHAR2(10),        -- 'March'
    quarter        NUMBER,              -- 1
    year           NUMBER,              -- 2026
    is_weekend     CHAR(1),             -- 'N'
    is_holiday     CHAR(1)              -- 'N'
);
💡 Why not just a DATE column? Because you can't GROUP BY is_holiday or filter by fiscal_quarter without a dimension.

Good news: ETL in this course = PL/SQL procedures. You already know how to do this!

ETL in PL/SQL


-- Load a dimension (denormalize with JOINs)
CREATE OR REPLACE PROCEDURE load_dim_doctor AS
BEGIN
    DELETE FROM dim_doctor;
    INSERT INTO dim_doctor (doctor_key, doctor_name, specialty, department)
    SELECT d.doctor_id, d.name, s.spec_name, dept.dept_name
    FROM Doctors d
    JOIN Specialties s ON d.specialty_id = s.spec_id
    JOIN Departments dept ON d.department_id = dept.dept_id;
    COMMIT;
    DBMS_OUTPUT.PUT_LINE('Loaded ' || SQL%ROWCOUNT || ' doctors');
END;
/
Key rule: Always load dimensions FIRST, then facts (facts need dimension FKs).

Part 6: Analytical SQL Preview

Deep dive next week — here's a taste

ROLLUP — Automatic Subtotals


SELECT NVL(d.dept_name, '** ALL **') AS department,
       NVL(doc.doctor_name, '** All Doctors **') AS doctor,
       SUM(f.total_cost) AS revenue
FROM fact_appointments f
JOIN dim_department d ON f.dept_key = d.dept_key
JOIN dim_doctor doc ON f.doctor_key = doc.doctor_key
GROUP BY ROLLUP(d.dept_name, doc.doctor_name);
        

DEPARTMENT    DOCTOR          REVENUE
Cardiology    Dr. Smith       $72,500
Cardiology    Dr. Jones       $49,000
Cardiology    ** All **       $121,500  ← subtotal
Neurology     Dr. Park        $100,500
** ALL **     ** All **       $300,000  ← grand total

RANK — Top-N Analysis


-- Top 3 doctors by revenue per department
SELECT * FROM (
    SELECT d.dept_name, doc.doctor_name,
           SUM(f.total_cost) AS revenue,
           RANK() OVER (
               PARTITION BY d.dept_name 
               ORDER BY SUM(f.total_cost) DESC
           ) AS rnk
    FROM fact_appointments f
    JOIN dim_department d ON f.dept_key = d.dept_key
    JOIN dim_doctor doc ON f.doctor_key = doc.doctor_key
    GROUP BY d.dept_name, doc.doctor_name
) WHERE rnk <= 3;
